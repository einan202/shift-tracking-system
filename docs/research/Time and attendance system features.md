# Building a time clock that beats commodity

A new time and attendance system must ship the commodity core (web, mobile and PIN-kiosk clocking, GPS stamps, break and overtime tracking, approvals, payroll export) and then compete on what incumbents do badly: reliable offline-first mobile, a transparent rules engine for labor-law compliance, privacy-safe identity verification, and honest pricing. Basic clocking is already free at the low end, since Jibble's free plan includes unlimited users, GPS and facial recognition, and Homebase's free tier covers phone and PIN clocking ([Jibble](https://www.jibble.io/features); [Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/time-clock)). Differentiation therefore moves to jurisdiction-aware rules (California meal-break premiums, predictive-scheduling laws, EU rest periods), integrations, and explainable anomaly detection. The biggest legal trap is biometrics: Illinois BIPA class actions and GDPR fines mean any face or fingerprint feature needs consent capture, retention controls and a non-biometric fallback. Typical SMB pricing is roughly $2-$12 per user per month, often with base fees, and review complaints cluster on hidden add-ons, weak mobile apps, GPS errors and missing offline mode. Caveat: most evidence comes from vendor pages and vendor-authored roundups, so claims are directional, and several figures conflict across sources.

## Table stakes are commoditizing, so the free tier sets the floor

Across Homebase, Deputy, Jibble, Clockify, When I Work, Rippling, BambooHR and the enterprise suites, the same core appears everywhere: clocking from web, mobile app and a shared tablet kiosk protected by a unique PIN. Timesheets are auto-generated with breaks, overtime is calculated and alerted, managers approve and edit punches, and data exports to payroll. GPS location stamps and geofencing are near-universal, though often gated to mid-priced tiers; Homebase, for example, puts photo capture and geofenced clock-in in Essentials and early-punch prevention and auto clock-out in Plus ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/time-clock)). Scheduling basics (open shifts, swaps with approval, availability and time-off requests, a basic labor-cost view) appear even at cheap tiers, such as Connecteam's $2 per employee per month Essentials tier ([Connecteam comparison](https://connecteam.com/homebase-vs-wheniwork/)). Leave management is similarly standardized: request and approve workflows, balances, and accrual rules by period, hours worked or tenure, with caps, carryover and waiting periods ([TimeTrakGO](https://www.timetrakgo.com/?p=3189); [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/pto-accrual/)). The notes' inference is that because Jibble bundles face recognition and GPS for free, these are being commoditized, and enterprise differentiation shifts to rules engines, hardware and integrations.

Break handling is a good example of where table stakes shade into differentiators. Homebase tracks paid, unpaid, missed and waived meal and rest breaks and auto-calculates missed-break pay ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/time-clock)), while Deputy auto-creates timesheets at shift end and alerts managers to missed breaks ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/features/time-clock)). Configurable rounding is the gap: it is absent from Deputy, Homebase and When I Work feature pages but standard in Jibble and the enterprise suites (UKG, Paycor), and a Timesheets.com reviewer complained that rounding was locked globally ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/132754/Timesheets-com/reviews/?page=7)).

| Layer | Features |
|---|---|
| Table stakes | Web, mobile and kiosk clocking; PIN; GPS stamp; geofence (mid tier); break tracking; auto timesheets; OT calculation and alerts; approvals and manager edits; payroll/CSV export; open shifts and swaps; PTO accrual and approval; offline capture on mobile and kiosk |
| Differentiators | Face verification with liveness checks; badge, fingerprint, QR/NFC hardware; Slack/Teams punches; auto clock-in on zone entry; attestation prompts; early-punch blocking; configurable rounding; multi-jurisdiction pay and OT rules; job costing; immutable edit audit trails; compliance-aware scheduling that blocks rather than warns; FMLA-style case management; anomaly detection |

## Differentiators cluster around identity, fraud control and rule engines

On identity, the enterprise vendors lead on hardware: ADP offers a mounted kiosk that works offline with facial recognition and an optional fingerprint scanner, and BambooHR's physical clock supports ID code, face, badge and fingerprint ([ADP](https://www.adp.com/what-we-offer/time-and-attendance/employee-time-clocks.aspx); [BambooHR](https://www.bamboohr.com/platform/time-and-attendance/)). Jibble is the broadest low-cost option, adding QR, NFC, desktop, a Chrome extension and Slack and Teams commands ([Jibble](https://www.jibble.io/features)). Anti-fraud differentiators include ADP's liveness detection and IP or area restrictions, Connecteam's geofence radius of 500-2,500 ft with manager approval requests for out-of-fence punches ([Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/employee-time-clock-app/time-theft/)), and Deputy's automatic attestation requests when someone is late, unscheduled, misses a break or leaves early ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/features/time-clock)). Only ADP and Jibble explicitly claim anti-spoofing, and the notes found no independent testing of liveness efficacy. A selfie saved for manager review is a different feature from true biometric matching, and the distinction matters legally.

On rules, the enterprise gap is depth. BambooHR supports federal, state and Canadian-province overtime rules or custom daily 1.5x, daily 2x and weekly 1.5x rules, and recalculates open timesheets when rules change ([BambooHR](https://www.bamboohr.com/product-updates/custom-overtime-rules-and-overtime-rules-for-canada)). Rippling applies prebuilt labor policies by work location with risk flags ([Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/time-and-attendance)). UKG's centralized rules engine projects likely violations before they occur ([UKG](https://www.ukg.com/solutions/ukg-dimensions-suite/compliance)). A tangible opportunity is premium-pay automation: Skytab's rule auto-applies one hour of pay for a missed rest break ([Skytab](https://intercom.help/skytabworkforce/en/articles/13157351-compliance-rule-missed-rest-break-penalty)), and Deputy flags timesheets owing premium pay and records break waivers ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/labor-compliance)). Audit trails are asserted by enterprise vendors but rarely detailed publicly. DCAA expects an audit trail of timesheet changes including approvals and rounding ([Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/6-considerations-for-employee-attendance-policies)), so an immutable, field-level edit history is both a differentiator and a procurement requirement.

Emerging AI features are real but easy to overclaim. Truein describes anomaly detection that combines capture-time controls (face verification, geofence) with post-capture pattern analysis across identity, location, device and event sequences, and cautions that a flag is a starting point for review, not proof of fraud ([Truein](https://truein.com/blogs/ai-anomaly-detection-time-tracking)). Legion's 2026 trends list continuously learning forecasting, skills-based scheduling and conversational assistants, though its cited Forrester ROI figure is vendor-commissioned ([Legion](https://legion.co/blog/workforce-management-trends-2026/)). The credible form of AI, in this evidence, is explainable flags with an evidence trail. The notes found nothing reliable on wearables, passive tracking, or independently measured chatbot and earned-wage-access adoption.

## Compliance is a patchwork, and biometrics carry the sharpest legal risk

The compliance burden depends on jurisdiction, so the system needs configurable rule packs rather than hard-coded logic. In California, an unpaid 30-minute meal break must start before the end of the fifth hour, a paid 10-minute rest is due per four hours, and each missed break type costs **one hour of premium pay per day**, treated as wages, with claims reaching back three years ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/navigating-californias-meal-rest-break-requirements); [DIR DLSE](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/restandmealperiods.pdf)). Predictive-scheduling laws exist statewide in Oregon (14 days notice) and in many cities, including NYC, Chicago, Seattle and Philadelphia, plus Los Angeles County from July 1, 2025. Jurisdiction counts differ by source and several states preempt local laws, so the rule data needs ongoing maintenance ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/time-matters-understanding-los-angeles-countys-new-fair-workweek-law)). Deputy's Fair Workweek module alerts on violating schedule changes and records employee consent, but it is limited to Premium and Enterprise plans ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/fair-workweek)). Minors' hours also need blocking or alerting; TCP/Humanity and Fourth hide restricted shifts from minors and warn as a shift nears its limit ([TCP](https://tcpsoftware.com/wp-content/uploads/tcp-humanity-minor-rules.pdf)). The US federal exempt salary level is back to **$684/week** after the 2024 rule was invalidated and removed ([Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/status-quo-dol-final-rule-keeps-overtime-salary-levels-2019-thresholds)). For leave, FMLA eligibility requires 12 months of employment and 1,250 hours worked, and the employer must issue a designation notice ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/28d-fmla-employer-notification)).

Outside the US, the EU Working Time Directive sets a 48-hour average week, 11 consecutive hours of daily rest and 24 hours of weekly rest, and CJEU case law requires systems that record daily working time ([Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/human-resources/working-hours-holiday-leave/working-hours/index_en.htm); [Dayforce](https://www.dayforce.com/uk/blog/eu-employers-recording-working-time-for-all-employees)). From 6 April 2026 the UK requires employers to keep records adequate to show compliance with annual leave and holiday pay, reportedly for six years and with criminal liability for failure, which turns leave audit trails into a compliance feature ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/03/27/failure-to-keep-adequate-holiday-records-will-become-a-criminal-offence-from-april-2026)). Israel requires a working-hours register with overtime at 125% then 150%, but the figures on caps, retention and daily rest conflict across the practitioner sources and must be checked against the statute and Ministry of Labor guidance ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2025/israel)).

Biometrics deserve their own design track. BIPA litigation remains active: a class certification was upheld against ITS in June 2025 (800+ workers, fingerprint and hand-geometry clocks), Speedway settled for **$12.1M**, and Accu-Time reached a $1.5M preliminary settlement ([IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/illinois-appeals-court-upholds-class-action-in-biometric-privacy-suit-against-its-technologies/); [Mealey's](https://www.mealeys.com/mealeys/articles/2377545)). A vendor, Anviz, has itself been sued over face-scan timekeeping ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/anviz-hit-with-biometric-suit-over-face-scan-timekeeping-system)). The 2024 amendment (SB 2979) limits recovery to one per person for repeated collection by the same method and allows electronic written releases, but exposure stays at $1,000-$5,000 per person, and the amendment's retroactivity was reported as contested ([Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/damage-control-illinois-enacts-amendment-states-high-risk-biometric-information)). In Europe, fingerprints are Article 9 special-category data, consent is weak in employment, and regulators expect less intrusive alternatives; the Dutch AP fined EUR 725,000 for processing without a demonstrated need ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/can-we-use-biometric-data-for-time-and-attendance-control-and-monitoring/); [Osborne Clarke](https://osborneclarke.com/insights/ap-imposes-gdpr-fine-processing-biometric-data-without-demonstrated-authentication-security-need)). The design implication is explicit consent and notice flows, retention and deletion schedules, and PIN, badge or QR fallback. Gaps remain: the notes did not source FLSA recordkeeping retention periods, Texas and Washington biometric laws, or SOC 2 expectations.

## Integrations decide adoption more than features do

Buyers expect payroll connectors (ADP, Gusto, Paychex, Paycor, Workday), accounting connectors (QuickBooks Online, Xero, Sage) and a CSV/API fallback. QuickBooks Time syncs natively with QuickBooks Online and Payroll, Deputy integrates with QuickBooks, Xero and ADP, and Homebase pairs built-in payroll with QuickBooks, Gusto and ADP ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/time-and-attendance-software)). Suite vendors win by owning one data model: Rippling links time, payroll, HR, IT and finance and claims 120+ integrations, while Dayforce unifies scheduling, time and payroll ([Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/time-and-attendance-software)). Marketplace add-ons show a viable partner lane: Oloid sells a face-recognition clock for ADP Workforce Now Essential Time ([ADP Marketplace](https://apps.adp.com/en-us/apps/330441/oloid-verify-time-clock-for-adp-workforce-now-essential-time/features)). POS clocking is another integration point, since Deputy can turn Clover, Square or Epos Now into a time clock ([Buddy Punch roundup](https://buddypunch.com/?p=29258)). A realistic launch set is QuickBooks Online, Xero, two or three payroll providers and robust CSV/API export, with SSO (SAML/OIDC/SCIM) and webhooks expected in the mid-market; the notes found no sourced detail on those, nor on SOC 2, MFA, multi-language, accessibility or multi-entity support, so these are unvalidated requirements. Reporting expectations are real-time labor cost, overtime and absenteeism dashboards with pre-payroll cost-spike alerts ([Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/time-and-attendance-software)); Deputy's reporting is described as high-level and Paycom's as limited for complex teams, which marks custom reporting as an opening. Convergence matters too: time tools keep adding payroll, invoicing and project management ([Programming Insider](https://programminginsider.com/from-time-tracking-to-work-intelligence-how-ai-is-changing-workforce-management-in-2026/)).

## Pain points and pricing show where to win

Review complaints are consistent. QuickBooks Time's mobile app and mileage or location accuracy draw criticism, Deputy's GPS inaccuracy can mark staff absent or mislocated, Buddy Punch reviewers cite slow apps, punches failing to register and no offline mode, and Capterra names missing offline functionality as a common issue ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/time-and-attendance-software); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/resources/time-management-software-problems/)). Enterprise tools draw setup complaints: UKG Ready reviewers report heavy setup effort and little assistance, and UKG Dimensions is rated 6.9/10 on TrustRadius, though G2's AI summary scores TimeClock Plus above UKG Pro on time and attendance and below it on forecasting ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/ukg-dimensions/reviews); [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/tcp-timeclock-plus-vs-ukg-pro)). Other recurring themes are limited manager permissions, rigid rounding, thin reporting, upselling, and privacy concerns about photos and location. Many of these complaints come from competitor-authored comparisons, so treat them as hypotheses, but together they imply the must-get-right list: offline-first mobile with later sync, accurate geofencing with a correction workflow, easy punch correction with an audit trail, flexible rounding and overtime rules, responsive support and transparent pricing.

Pricing is dominated by per-user monthly fees plus feature tiers, with free entry tiers. Reported figures come from roundups and conflict at times, and enterprise suites (UKG, Workday, ADP, Dayforce) are quote-only.

| Vendor | Reported pricing |

A competitive SMB band is about **$3-$10 per user per month** with a free tier for 5-10 users, and the notes flag that base fees, required companion subscriptions and add-on modules raise real cost (Rippling costs rise with modules, ADP uses add-ons). Prices change often and should be re-verified before positioning.

## Conclusion

The evidence suggests that no one wins by adding more clocking methods; the contest is over trust. Free tiers have made punching cheap, while the costly failures are legal (biometric class actions, missed premium pay, predictive-scheduling penalties) and operational (offline gaps, GPS errors, opaque edits). A new entrant can credibly pair a transparent, jurisdiction-configurable rules engine and immutable audit trail with privacy-by-default identity verification, where non-biometric methods are the default and biometrics are a consented option.

Open questions limit how far this can be taken. The notes lack independent liveness testing, verified enterprise pricing, primary Reddit or G2 review data, and sourced detail on SSO, webhooks, SOC 2 and accessibility. Israel-specific rules need verification against the statute if that market matters. These should be closed with demos, primary legal sources and customer interviews before scoping the build.
