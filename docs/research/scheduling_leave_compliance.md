# Scheduling, Leave/Absence Management, and Labor-Law Compliance Features of Time and Attendance Systems

Note: search-tool summaries were mostly vendor/law-firm blogs; vendor claims are self-reported. Several figures conflict across sources and are flagged.

## 1. What scheduling features exist (templates, drag-drop, swap/open shifts, availability, auto-scheduling, forecasting, labor budgeting)?

### Takeaway
Shift swap/open shifts and labor-cost tracking are advertised even by SMB tools (Homebase, When I Work, Connecteam). Rule-enforced auto-scheduling, compliance-aware scheduling (fair workweek, minors, rest-time rules) and predictive violation alerts are concentrated in mid/enterprise tiers (Deputy Premium/Enterprise, UKG, TCP/Humanity, Fourth).

### Cited Findings
- Homebase lists open shifts, shift swapping and labor cost forecasting/controls; its schedule builder shows real-time labor cost vs budget while building the schedule; per-location pricing vs When I Work per-user pricing (vendor claim) — [Homebase vs When I Work](https://www.joinhomebase.com/compare/homebase-vs-wheniwork); [Homebase vs Connecteam](https://www.joinhomebase.com/comparison/connecteam)
- Connecteam: swap/release/pick-up shifts at its $2/employee/month Essentials tier; sales and labor budget feature in a custom-priced tier; Connecteam claims (against Homebase and HotSchedules) it can enforce scheduling rules like minimum rest time between shifts and max weekly hours — [Connecteam comparison](https://connecteam.com/homebase-vs-wheniwork/)
- When I Work: open shift posting and swaps with manager approval; sources disagree on labor budgeting (Homebase rates it "Limited"; Connecteam lists budgeting in the $24/location/month Essentials tier) — [Connecteam comparison](https://connecteam.com/homebase-vs-wheniwork/); [Homebase comparison](https://www.joinhomebase.com/compare/homebase-vs-wheniwork)
- Deputy: auto-scheduling with customizable rules to meet business and compliance requirements; Fair Workweek tool alerts on creating/changing published shifts that violate local legislation; records employee consent for last-minute changes, suggests predictability pay; Fair Workweek module available on Premium and Enterprise — [Deputy Fair Workweek](https://www.deputy.com/fair-workweek); [Deputy help](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/8433876310543)
- UKG Dimensions (now "UKG Pro Workforce Management"): centralized rules engine for simple and complex pay/work rules without customization; real-time projections predict possible violations before they occur — [UKG buyer's guide](https://www.ukg.com/blog/operations-leaders/how-workforce-management-software-supports-labor-law-compliance-practical-buyers-guide); [UKG compliance](https://www.ukg.com/solutions/ukg-dimensions-suite/compliance)
- Minor-rule scheduling: vendors (TCP/Humanity, Fourth) block or warn on shifts violating age-based hours, hide restricted shifts from minors, warn when a minor's shift nears its end, accommodate waivers — [TCP Humanity minor rules](https://tcpsoftware.com/wp-content/uploads/tcp-humanity-minor-rules.pdf); [Fourth](https://www.fourth.com/solution/workforce-management/scheduling/compliance)
- Not found in sources: specific demand-forecasting methods (POS/traffic-based ML), shift-template/drag-drop specifics, ADP/Paycom/Rippling scheduling feature detail. See Gaps.

### Inferences
- Table-stakes (even free/cheap tiers): publish schedule, mobile access, open shifts, swap with approval, availability/time-off requests, basic labor-cost view. Differentiators: automated rule-based scheduling, compliance-aware blocking (not only warnings), forecasting-driven schedules, fair-workweek consent/premium-pay automation, minor-law enforcement. (Inference from vendor tier placement.)

### Gaps
- Forecasting/labor-demand modeling details, shift templates, drag-and-drop, availability management, and ADP/Paycom/Rippling/Connecteam first-party feature pages were not retrieved.

## 2. What leave/PTO/absence features exist (accruals, approvals, holidays, sick leave, FMLA-type tracking, alerts)?

### Takeaway
Configurable accrual (by period/hours worked/tenure), caps, carryover, waiting periods and jurisdictional sick-leave minimums are standard; FMLA-type case management with eligibility logic and auto-populated DOL forms is a more specialized/enterprise feature.

### Cited Findings
- Accrual rules configurable by year, month, pay period, hour worked or percentage; separate rules for sick/vacation/floating holidays and by role, location, tenure; per-rule carryover limits or use-it-or-lose-it; accrual caps; waiting periods before accrual/use — [TimeTrakGO](https://www.timetrakgo.com/?p=3189); [Homebase PTO update](https://joinhomebase.com/product-update/even-more-control-on-paid-time-off-and-paid-sick-leave-policies/); [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/pto-accrual/)
- State sick-leave minimums must still be met in a consolidated PTO bank; example: Washington accrues 1 hr per 40 hrs worked, no annual accrual cap, up to 40 hrs carryover, employers may require 90-day wait before use — [Paychex](https://www.paychex.com/articles/human-resources/does-pto-policy-make-sense); [Gusto](https://gusto.com/product/time-tools/absence-management). (One source claimed NY has no accrual cap; the search summarizer flagged this as likely wrong - do not rely.)
- UKG: enforces rules for paid/unpaid leave across federal, state and employer policies, syncs with scheduling and payroll; UKG Ready Leave Manager lists flexible leave rules, eligibility criteria, timesheet/schedule integration, auto-populated DOL forms; employee access to accrual balances emphasized — [UKG Ready Leave Manager profile](https://www.ukg.com/sites/default/files/2023-10/UKG%20Ready%20Leave%20Manager%20Product%20Profile_v6.pdf); [UKG buyer's guide](https://www.ukg.com/blog/operations-leaders/how-workforce-management-software-supports-labor-law-compliance-practical-buyers-guide)
- FMLA (US) eligibility: 12 months employment, 1,250 hours worked in prior 12 months (paid/unpaid leave not counted), worksite with 50+ employees within 75 miles; employer must designate leave and issue designation notice (one per qualifying reason per leave year; late notice may count as interference) — [SHRM toolkit](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/tools/toolkits/managing-family-medical-leave-fmla); [DOL fact sheet 28D](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/28d-fmla-employer-notification)
- UK: from 6 April 2026 the Employment Rights Act 2025 (s.35 amending Working Time Regulations) requires employers to keep records adequate to show compliance with annual leave and holiday pay; sources say 6-year retention and criminal offence for failure; format at employer's discretion; statutory 5.6 weeks unchanged; reg number (16A vs 16B) conflicts — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/03/27/failure-to-keep-adequate-holiday-records-will-become-a-criminal-offence-from-april-2026); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/04/united-kingdom-new-record-keeping-obligations-for-annual-leave); [Global Workplace Insider](https://www.globalworkplaceinsider.com/2026/03/employers-duty-to-record-annual-leave-and-pay/)

### Inferences
- The UK 2026 holiday-record duty makes leave/accrual audit trails (including irregular-hours holiday calculation) a compliance feature, not just convenience.
- Table-stakes: request/approve workflow, balances, accrual rules, calendar view. Differentiators: multi-jurisdiction sick-leave rule packs, FMLA/intermittent-leave case management, holiday-pay calculation for variable hours.

### Gaps
- Late/absence alerts, no-show detection, holiday calendar features, Israel-specific leave (sick/convalescence) and rolling-year FMLA calculation were not sourced. FMLA software capability claims were not found.

## 3. What compliance features exist and which laws drive them?

### Takeaway
Requirements are driven by a patchwork: FLSA recordkeeping/overtime (US), state meal/rest rules (esp. California), local predictive-scheduling laws, EU/UK working-time rules, Israeli hours law, and biometric/data privacy (BIPA, GDPR). Vendors respond with rules engines, pre-violation alerts, attestations, premium-pay automation and audit trails.

### Cited Findings
**US federal / state**
- Federal exempt salary level is $684/week ($35,568/yr) (2019 level); DOL announced removal of the 2024 overtime rule from the CFR in May 2026 after court invalidation; some states set higher thresholds; salary test alone doesn't determine exemption — [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/compensation-trends/flsa-exemption-rules-2026-what-changed-on-january-1st-and-what-hr-needs-to-know); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/public-accommodations-accessibility/polsinelli-at-work-blog/dol-rolls-back-2024-biden-era-overtime-rule); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/status-quo-dol-final-rule-keeps-overtime-salary-levels-2019-thresholds)
- California: unpaid 30-min meal break starting before end of 5th hour; second meal by end of 10th hour on days over 10 hrs; paid 10-min rest per 4 hrs or major fraction; one hour premium pay at regular rate per day per break type (max 2 hrs/day); premium is wages (2022 CA Supreme Court); claims reach back 3 years — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/navigating-californias-meal-rest-break-requirements); [DIR DLSE](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/restandmealperiods.pdf)
- Vendor break features: Deputy schedules breaks, flags missed breaks, records waiver agreement, flags timesheets owing premium pay, asks meal-attestation question after 5+ hrs for non-waived employees; Skytab "missed rest break penalty" rule auto-applies 1 hr pay to timecard — [Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/labor-compliance); [Deputy help](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/7791383067919); [Skytab](https://intercom.help/skytabworkforce/en/articles/13157351-compliance-rule-missed-rest-break-penalty)
- Predictive scheduling: Oregon is the only statewide law (14 days notice since 2020; one source cites $500-$2,000 penalties per employee per day); local laws include NYC, Chicago, Seattle, Philadelphia, SF, LA city, Berkeley, San Jose, Emeryville, and LA County (effective July 1, 2025); counts of jurisdictions differ across sources (8 vs 10+); several states preempt local laws (lists differ: ~11 states); Berkeley penalty $50/employee/day; large 2025-26 penalties in Seattle and NYC reported; MI, CT, RI considering state bills (pending) — [Homebase guide](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws); [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws); [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/predictive-work-schedule-laws); [Littler on LA County](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/time-matters-understanding-los-angeles-countys-new-fair-workweek-law)
- Minors: federal age bands (under 14 largely prohibited, 14-15 detailed hour limits, 16-17 no federal hour limits but hazardous-occupation bans); state rules may be stricter (Utah example: 9:30 vs federal 9:00); software blocks/alerts/hides shifts — [TCP](https://tcpsoftware.com/wp-content/uploads/tcp-humanity-minor-rules.pdf); [WorkforceHub](https://www.workforcehub.com/blog/a-cautionary-tale-for-employers-16k-dol-fine-for-city-department-of-recreation/)
**Biometrics / privacy**
- Illinois BIPA: SB 2979 signed Aug 2, 2024 limits recovery to one per person for repeated collection by same method (responding to Cothron v. White Castle per-scan accrual), and allows electronic signature as written release; Seventh Circuit reported to apply it retroactively (contested earlier; verify status); exposure remains $1,000-$5,000 per person — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/damage-control-illinois-enacts-amendment-states-high-risk-biometric-information); [Duane Morris](https://dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/08/illinois-bipa-biometrics-law-amended-for-damages); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/ko/insights/2024/8/bipa-update-illinois-limits-liability-and-clarifies-electronic-consent-for-biometric-data-collection)
- GDPR/UK GDPR: fingerprints are Art. 9 special-category data; need Art. 6 basis plus Art. 9(2) exception; consent weak in employment; regulators expect less intrusive alternatives (card/PIN); fines: Dutch AP EUR 725,000 (no demonstrated need), Belgian DPA EUR 45,000; UK ICO guidance and Serco case — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/can-we-use-biometric-data-for-time-and-attendance-control-and-monitoring/); [Osborne Clarke](https://osborneclarke.com/insights/ap-imposes-gdpr-fine-processing-biometric-data-without-demonstrated-authentication-security-need); [PwC Belgium](https://www.pwclegal.be/en/news/belgian-data-protection-authority-fines-employer--45-000-for-unl.html); [Taylor Wessing](https://globaldatahub.taylorwessing.com/article/processing-employee-fingerprint-data)
**EU / UK / Israel**
- EU Working Time Directive: avg 48 hrs/week incl. overtime (opt-out possible; reference period up to 4/6/12 months), 11 consecutive hours daily rest, 24 hrs weekly rest; entire on-call stand-by counts as working time in some cases; CJEU case law requires systems to record daily working time; Commission notes most Member States require registration, except BE, DK, CY, MT, SE which lack/unclear (secondary commentary; CJEU case citation (CCOO, C-55/18) not retrieved in this run) — [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/human-resources/working-hours-holiday-leave/working-hours/index_en.htm); [EC](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/rights-work/labour-law/working-conditions/working-time-directive_en); [Dayforce](https://www.dayforce.com/uk/blog/eu-employers-recording-working-time-for-all-employees)
- Israel (Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951; sources are practitioner/vendor blogs, unverified against statute): register of working hours required, ~3-year retention, 42-hr standard week, daily cap ~12 hrs incl. overtime, overtime 125% first 2 hrs then 150%, 36-hr weekly rest; weekly overtime caps conflict across sources (12/15 vs 16 hrs; 47-hr total) and daily-rest figure conflicts (8 vs 12 hrs) — [Chambers Employment 2025 Israel](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2025/israel); [Harvest](https://www.getharvest.com/time-tracking/timesheet-software-in-israel); [Rivermate](https://rivermate.com/guides/israel/working-hours)
**Vendor compliance engine claims**
- UKG: centralized rules engine, pre-violation projections; Deputy: alerts on schedule create/edit, consent capture, compliance records stored multi-year; Connecteam: min-rest and max-hours enforcement; TCP/Fourth: minor rules — see sources above.

### Inferences
- Table-stakes: accurate timestamped time records, overtime calculation, basic break rules, edit audit trail (typical expectation; not specifically sourced here). Differentiators: jurisdiction rule packs (fair workweek, CA premium pay), blocking vs warning, attestation capture, biometric consent workflows (BIPA/GDPR), minors engine, EU rest-period (11 hr) enforcement.
- Biometric clocks are higher legal risk in EU (necessity test) than Illinois post-2024; vendors typically need consent capture/retention schedules and non-biometric fallback.

### Gaps
- FLSA 29 CFR 516 retention periods (commonly 3 yrs payroll / 2 yrs time cards) not sourced in this run; verify at eCFR/DOL.
- SOC 2, data retention/audit-trail specifics, Texas CUBI / Washington biometric laws, other overtime regimes (CA daily OT), not sourced.
- Israel figures need statute/Ministry of Labor check. Specific CJEU judgment citation not retrieved. Vendor pages for ADP, Paycom, Rippling compliance features not retrieved.
